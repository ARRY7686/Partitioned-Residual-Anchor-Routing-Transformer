# 07 — High resolution

**Question.** On images where downsampling destroys the signal, do focus anchors beat the fixed
partition, dense attention with local windows, and ViTs on downsampled inputs? And at what
resolution, if any, do anchors save memory or time against dense attention with a fused
(FlashAttention) kernel?

**Status:** done, 3 seeds, on 2× A100-80GB (2026-11-02/03, bf16). Every number below is in
[`../RESULTS_SUMMARY.md`](../RESULTS_SUMMARY.md), generated by `../summarize_results.py`.

## Findings

1. **Downsampling halves accuracy on real full-HD images.** On DFG traffic signs, a ViT on a
   480-pixel-wide input reaches 15.8 mAP, against 28.6–30.3 for every full-resolution transformer.
2. **Anchors match dense attention at about a fifth of its compute.** On DFG, focus (30.28) and
   fixed (29.62) anchors are level with dense attention with local windows (30.28) and the dense
   ViT (28.60), at 187 against 846–912 GFLOPs per image. On Megapixel MNIST, fixed anchors and
   dense-with-windows are level (91.9 against 91.4).
3. **Focus and fixed anchors are not distinguishable at high resolution** (DFG: +0.67 mAP,
   95% CI [−1.90, +3.23]). The focus advantage is a Cluttered CIFAR-10 result (experiment 06).
4. **Anchors train faster than FlashAttention only at high resolution, and never use less
   memory.** Fixed anchors take 1.5× less time per training step at full HD and 2.3× less at
   2048²; focus anchors are only faster at 2048². Against attention without a fused kernel, the
   dense ViT runs out of memory from 1536² at batch 8.
5. **A CNN is still best.** ResNet-18 leads on both tasks (DFG 36.5 mAP, Megapixel MNIST 99.3%).
   PVTv2-B0 does well on Megapixel MNIST (97.4%) but poorly on DFG (19.5).

## Tasks

| Task | Images | Classes / metric | Why downsampling hurts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Megapixel MNIST (`mpmnist`), after Katharopoulos & Fleuret (2019) | 512² canvas; 10,000 fresh training layouts per epoch / 1000 val / 1000 test | 10, accuracy | Five native 28 px digits (three of the target class, two of another) among 12 stroke-like noise patches. At 128² a digit is 7 px. |
| DFG traffic signs (`dfg`), Tabernik & Skočaj (2019) | 1920×1080 street scenes (padded to 1920×1152); 4480 train / 774 val / 1703 test frames | 20 most frequent sign types, multi-label mAP | Median sign 134 px wide, 10% under 45 px. At 480 px wide a small sign is a few pixels. |

Details:

* **Megapixel MNIST.** Our generator follows the original recipe, at 512² instead of 1500² so
  the canvas divides into 16 px patches and 8×8 windows. The noise patches are our own: one or two
  random line segments each, 12 per canvas (the density of 50 on a 1024² canvas). Validation
  digits come from MNIST training images 50,000–59,999, test digits from the MNIST test set;
  validation and test layouts are fixed, training layouts are redrawn every epoch.
* **First attempt, 1024² with 5000 fixed layouts:** focus anchors stayed at chance (11.6% after
  10 epochs, training loss at ln 10), so we moved to 512² with fresh layouts. Those logs are kept.
* **DFG.** The official train/test split is kept. It interleaves frames from the same video
  sequences, so train and test images can be near-duplicates. The validation split takes every
  7th block of 100 consecutive frame numbers from the training images. Sign annotations flagged
  `ignore` are excluded from the loss and the mAP. No augmentation, because flipping would change
  the meaning of some signs.
* **DFG needs max pooling.** With the usual global *average* pooling, every model stayed near
  chance (8–12 mAP; chance is 3.1), including an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 (8.7). The
  pipeline itself was checked: the mAP code matches scikit-learn, boxes align with signs on
  rendered frames, and a pretrained ResNet-18 memorises 64 training frames to 100 mAP. With *max*
  pooling the pretrained ResNet-18 reaches 47.8 mAP in 6 epochs, so averaging over a full-HD frame
  was washing out the signs. All DFG comparisons therefore use max-pooled heads
  (`RART_POOL=max`) for every model.

Settings were fixed in advance and every run reports validation and test scores per epoch:

* transformers and PVTv2: AdamW, learning rate 2e-4, batch 32;
* ResNet-18: learning rate 1e-3;
* all models: gradient clip 1.0, 2-epoch warmup, cosine decay, bf16;
* epochs: 30 for Megapixel MNIST and 20 for DFG.

## Models

| Name | What it is |
|---|---|
| `focus_A64` | 16 coverage anchors (4×4) and 48 focus anchors, exclusive Voronoi regions |
| `fixed_A64` | 64 anchors on a fixed 8×8 grid (Partition RART) |
| `densewin` | The same blocks with the anchor pathway replaced by global attention among all patches, local windows kept |
| `vit_native` | Dense ViT on the full-resolution input |
| `vit256`, `vit128` / `vit480` | Dense ViT on an area-downsampled input (long side in pixels) |
| `pvt`, `resnet18_lr1e-3` | PVTv2-B0 and ResNet-18 (`timm`, no pretraining) |

The patch grids are rectangular. Anchor positions are measured in units of the long side, so
Voronoi regions stay isotropic.

## Results

### Megapixel MNIST 512², 30 epochs (phase N)

| Model | Test accuracy (%) |
|---|---:|
| ResNet-18 | 99.30 ± 0.26 |
| PVTv2-B0 | 97.43 ± 0.21 |
| ViT, 256² input (digits 14 px) | 93.37 ± 2.40 |
| fixed-64 | 91.87 ± 0.35 |
| dense with windows | 91.40 ± 0.20 |
| focus-64 (seeds 43, 44; seed 42 below) | 91.15 ± 0.92 |
| ViT, 128² input (digits 7 px) | 87.50 ± 1.90 |
| dense ViT, 512² | 86.10 ± 0.96 |

Paired gaps (95% CI): fixed − dense with windows +0.47 [−0.25, +1.18]; fixed − dense ViT
+5.77 [+4.02, +7.51]; fixed − ViT 128² +4.37 [+0.47, +8.27]; ViT 256² − fixed
+1.50 [−3.60, +6.60]. At 512² the digits are still legible at half resolution, so this task
rewards local windows more than resolution itself.

**Focus instability.** At learning rate 2e-4, focus seed 42 became unstable at epoch 13 (gradient
median from about 5 to over 1000) and ended at 79.0%. Training is deterministic: a rerun
reproduced it exactly. The same seed at learning rate 1e-4 trained cleanly (88.7%, no gradient
spikes); with 16 anchors at 2e-4 it was worse (64.4%). The spikes start in the patch embedding
and the first block's local attention, with the focus importance head among the largest
gradients. Seeds 43 and 44 were stable at 2e-4.

### Cost (phase C; A100, bf16, untrained, alone on the GPU)

Training step at batch 8, ms (peak training memory, GB):

| Resolution | Dense ViT, FlashAttention | Dense ViT, plain attention | fixed-64 | focus-64 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 512² | 11.5 (0.9) | 44.8 (2.6) | 44.7 (1.4) | 196.9 (1.5) |
| 1024² | 58.9 (3.0) | 548.6 (30.0) | 68.5 (4.8) | 196.7 (5.3) |
| 1536² | 198.4 (6.3) | out of memory | 134.9 (10.5) | 296.2 (11.5) |
| 2048² | 533.6 (11.1) | out of memory | 232.1 (18.5) | 288.2 (20.2) |
| 1920×1152 | 187.9 (6.4) | out of memory | 128.0 (10.4) | 204.2 (11.3) |

The sequential placement of 48 focus anchors adds 56–161 ms per training step over fixed anchors
at batch 8. It does not grow with resolution, so it dominates at small sizes, and focus anchors
only overtake FlashAttention at 2048².

## Running

On a rented GPU:

```bash
bash setup_box.sh                                       # deps, MNIST, DFG (7.5 GB), smoke tests
RART_PHASES=C RART_JOBS_PER_GPU=1 python run_suite.py   # cost sweep, alone
RART_PHASES=N RART_JOBS_PER_GPU=2 python run_suite.py   # Megapixel MNIST 512², fresh layouts
RART_PHASES=DM RART_JOBS_PER_GPU=1 python run_suite.py  # DFG, max-pooled heads
```

Phases M and D (1024² Megapixel MNIST, average-pooled DFG) are the first attempts described
above; their results are kept as `D_*` and `results/diag/`. `RART_PHASES=NF` reruns focus seeds
42 and 44. The launch scripts actually used are in `results/`.

`build_data.py dfg --limit-mb 200` fetches only the first part of the image archive, for a
local smoke test. On Windows, set `RART_WORKERS=0` for DFG, because data-loader worker processes
re-import the script.

| Variable | Meaning | Default |
|---|---|---|
